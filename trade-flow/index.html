If your group shares trades on WhatsApp, this tracks every idea.

TradeFlow turns a WhatsApp group into a structured trade tape — what the crowd is trading, who's active, and how those calls played out. Runs on your hardware. Read-only: it watches and scores, never trades.

The basics

What this is, in plain terms.

The problem

Ideas get buried in chat.

Trade ideas are posted in a group chat and scroll out of view. Weeks later it's hard to find who suggested what, or whether the idea worked.

Who it's for

Members of trading group chats.

For groups that share stock, options, or futures ideas in chat and want a record of what was suggested and how it turned out.

What it's for

A record of ideas and outcomes.

It lists what the group traded, who posted, and how each idea performed five trading days later.

What it's not

Limitations.

Not a live monitor. Data refreshes hourly; it's for review, not for reacting in real time.

Not a trading bot. Read-only, no broker connection, places no trades.

Not financial advice. It reports what happened; it doesn't recommend what to do.

Overview

What it shows.

01

What did the group trade?

One chronological tape of actual trades, minus the questions and chatter around them. Filter by symbol, instrument, action, outcome, or trader.

02

Where is the crowd leaning?

Whether the group is opening or closing, put/call balance, the busiest symbols, and names several members are piling into.

03

Did the call work?

Every call gets a five-trading-day direction check: favorable, unfavorable, or flat. Stated targets are tracked separately. Options are scored on the stock's direction, not the contract's profit.

04

Who contributes useful flow?

The Traders view compares activity, active days, favorite symbols, and scored outcomes. Nobody's identity is exposed, and small samples stay labeled.

Outcome scoring

Every call, checked against the market.

Each action is scored against real daily market data — TradingView or Yahoo Finance, your pick. The scores show up on per-trader scorecards and in crowd insights.

Real market data

  • Five-trading-day window after the call, on real daily bars
  • Moves inside a 1% band count as flat — the rest are favorable or unfavorable
  • Stated targets are watched for 14 days
  • Options scored on underlying direction — never fictional premium P&L

Trader scorecards

  • Per-member cards: activity, favorite symbols, instrument mix
  • Scored ✓ / ✗ / – record, plus target hits
  • Fully anonymized — Trader 01, Trader 02, and so on
  • Small samples stay labeled, so a 2-for-2 streak never masquerades as a track record

Crowd insights

  • Deterministic rules, no black boxes: activity spikes and drops, new-trader crowds
  • Put/call sentiment shifts, open/close lean flips, options-share surges
  • Hot traders flagged at 5+ scored calls with a 60%+ hit rate
  • Top signals surface first — and when it’s calm, it says so
Privacy by design

It runs on your machine.

Raw chat and WhatsApp identities never reach the dashboard. Each message goes to the language model you pick for extraction, and only structured, pseudonymized trade records get stored.

  • Stable pseudonyms replace names and phone numbers
  • Secrets and the WhatsApp session stay on your machine
  • Hourly pull-and-disconnect — no permanent session
  • Every data boundary is inspectable in the source

How it works

The whole pipeline, in the open.

The repo has the reader, parser, outcome scorer, setup UI, database schema, and dashboard. It runs on your own hardware; a free Oracle Cloud VM is enough.

01

Pull

Connect as a linked device, fetch messages since the last checkpoint, disconnect. Hourly 8:30am–5pm ET, plus a midnight run.

02

Parse

Split buys, sells, plans, options, and quoted context into structured trade actions.

03

Score

Check five-trading-day direction against market data.

04

Explore

Review flow, outcomes, clusters, and trader activity on the dashboard.

The stack

Every layer is swappable.

Chat reader

whatsapp-web.js

Connects as a linked device, pulls messages since the last checkpoint, and disconnects. No permanent session, no spare phone.

Language model

Gemini API · free tier

Turns chat messages into structured trade records: symbol, action, instrument, strike, expiry, quoted context. Any OpenAI-compatible LLM works.

Market data

TradingView

Daily bars for the five-day outcome scoring, round-trip detection, and target checks. Yahoo Finance (free, no key) is a drop-in alternative.

Database

Supabase · free tier

Postgres stores the pseudonymized trade records and sync metadata. The dashboard reads it live; row-level security keeps writes server-side. Any PostgREST-compatible database works.

Runtime

Node.js + Python

Node drives the WhatsApp reader and setup UI; Python handles extraction, scoring, and the database push. It all runs on your VM — one shell script per cycle, fired hourly by a systemd timer.

Hosting

Vercel + Oracle Cloud · free

The dashboard is a dependency-free static site on Vercel; the pipeline runs on an Oracle Cloud Always Free VM. Any Ubuntu VM works as the host.

The above is the free-tier default, not a requirement.

The setup prompt

Set up TradeFlow end to end: https://github.com/vibhortayal/wa-trade-bot WHAT IT IS: an hourly pipeline on a VM. A WhatsApp linked-device reader pulls new messages from one trading group, an LLM (Gemini by default, any OpenAI-compatible API works) extracts structured trade actions, market data (TradingView by default, Yahoo Finance works too) scores each outcome over five trading days, and pseudonymized records land in Postgres (Supabase by default, any PostgREST endpoint works) for a static dashboard. PREREQUISITES — I have all of these ready: - Oracle Cloud Always Free account (any Ubuntu VM works; Oracle is the free suggestion) - Ubuntu 24.04 ARM VM (Ampere A1: 4 OCPUs / 24 GB RAM) with a public IP; subnet security list allows TCP ingress on port 3001 from 0.0.0.0/0 - My WhatsApp account on my phone (I can type in a pairing code) - Gemini API key (free tier; or any OpenAI-compatible LLM base URL + key + model) - Supabase project URL + service_role key (or any PostgREST endpoint) - TradingView account (optional — for outcome scoring; or use free Yahoo Finance instead) - Vercel account (free tier — hosts the dashboard) DO THIS IN ORDER. Verify each step before moving on. 1. DEPLOY THE CODE Copy the repo to the VM. The VM directory is NOT a git repo — always deploy with scp, never git pull there: scp -r . ubuntu@<VM-IP>:~/wa-trade-bot SSH in and install (Node 20, Python 3, Chromium, systemd units). It asks for a setup-UI admin password — have me choose one: ssh ubuntu@<VM-IP> bash ~/wa-trade-bot/deploy/install.sh 2. OPEN THE SETUP UI http://<VM-IP>:3001/setup.html — HTTP Basic Auth, any username + the admin password from step 1. (Plain HTTP: fine for setup over a trusted network; don't expose it more broadly than needed.) 3. LINK WHATSAPP In the UI, enter my phone number in international format (e.g. 15551234567) and get the pairing code — hand it to me immediately, codes expire. I type it into WhatsApp → Settings → Linked devices → "Link with phone number instead". Confirm the UI reports wa_paired: true before continuing. 4. SAVE THE KEYS In the UI, paste the Gemini API key, the Supabase URL, and the Supabase service_role key. Then run the SQL shown in the UI's schema box once in the Supabase SQL editor — it creates the wa_trades and wa_meta tables with anon read-only access. Leave GEMINI_MODEL unset; the default model is used. 5. SET THE GROUP In the setup UI, enter the trading group's name in the "WhatsApp group to watch" field and save (it also shows the current value, and you can change or reset it any time). Alternatively, set WA_GROUP_QUERY in ~/wa-trade-bot/.env over SSH. (Substring match on the WhatsApp group name; the reader only touches group chats and stays within this one group.) 6. CONNECT TRADINGVIEW (outcome scoring) In the UI: pick the market-data source first. For TradingView, start the auth, open the authorize URL, approve it, then paste the full callback URL (the one containing ?code=...) back into the UI -- tokens stay on the VM. For Yahoo Finance there is nothing to connect. Skip this if I don't need outcome scoring. 7. RUN THE FIRST CYCLE In the UI press "Run a cycle now" — or over SSH: MANUAL_RUN=1 bash ~/wa-trade-bot/run-cycle.sh It pulls recent messages, parses trades, scores outcomes, and pushes to Supabase. Verify the cycle exits 0 and new rows appear in the wa_trades table. 8. CONFIRM THE SCHEDULE sudo systemctl status wa-trade-bot-cycle.timer — the hourly pipeline runs 08:30–17:00 America/New_York plus a midnight ET run. 9. DEPLOY THE DASHBOARD Vercel is the dashboard's canonical home — the bot only runs the pipeline and the setup UI. Edit dashboard/supabase-config.js with your Supabase project URL and anon key, commit, and push. In Vercel, import the repo with the root directory set to dashboard/ — it redeploys automatically on every push. Open the Vercel URL; it reads live from Supabase. HARD RULES - Never print, log, or commit: .env contents, data/messages.jsonl (raw chat), data/pseudonyms.json, phone numbers, sender names, group IDs, API keys, the admin password, or pairing codes. They are gitignored — keep them that way. - No git pull on the VM. Ever. Deploy with scp. - PUPPETEER_EXECUTABLE_PATH must be /snap/chromium/current/usr/lib/chromium-browser/chrome (the real binary). /snap/bin/chromium does NOT work inside systemd services. - Keep whatsapp-web.js's default Chrome/101 user agent; newer user agents land on WhatsApp's unsupported-browser page. - If anything fails, show me the exact error plus the log tail (the UI "Status" section, or logs/cycle.log on the VM) before retrying.

Questions people actually ask

Does it read my personal chats?

No. It watches exactly one group chat — the trading group you name in the setup UI — and only group messages. Your 1:1 chats are never touched.

Do I need a spare phone or SIM?

No. The reader pairs as a linked device on your existing WhatsApp account with a one-time code, then disconnects after every hourly pull.

Does it place trades?

Never. It's read-only analytics — it watches, structures, and scores. There is no brokerage connection and no order path anywhere in the code.

What does it cost to run?

$0 a month on free tiers: an Oracle Cloud Always Free VM, the Gemini free tier, Supabase free tier, and Vercel free tier. Every layer is swappable if you'd rather use your own providers.

Things that broke for us.

Pairing never completes

Pairing is a round trip: the setup UI shows a code, you type it into WhatsApp on your phone, and WhatsApp confirms back to the VM. The link only counts once the UI reports wa_paired: true. Type the code in right away — codes expire within minutes — and wait for that confirmation before moving on. If it keeps failing, the VM is holding a stale half-paired session: click "Get a fresh code" in the setup UI, which wipes it and starts clean.

Can't reach the setup UI on port 3001

The page at http://<VM-IP>:3001/setup.html won't load. Oracle blocks all ports by default — the subnet's security list needs a TCP ingress rule for port 3001 (source 0.0.0.0/0). Add it in the Oracle console under Networking → Security Lists; the checklist above has the exact rule.

Oracle says "out of capacity" when creating the VM

Always Free capacity in a region comes and goes — we hit this in us-sanjose-1 for both free shapes. Retry, or try the other shape. What unblocked us: upgrading the tenancy to Pay As You Go (card on file, still free-tier eligible) with a budget alert as a safety net.

The reader pulls no messages

Two usual causes. First, a fresh linked device takes up to a minute to sync its chat list — the reader polls and waits, so give it a moment and run the cycle again. Second, the group filter: the reader only looks at the group named in "WhatsApp group to watch" in the setup UI (case-insensitive substring match). If that doesn't match the group's actual name in WhatsApp, it finds nothing.

The reader fails: Chromium won't start

Symptom: pairing codes never appear, or reads die instantly. The chromium snap's launcher wrapper refuses to run inside a systemd service — the browser starts fine over SSH but never from the timer or setup UI. The fix is PUPPETEER_EXECUTABLE_PATH=/snap/chromium/current/usr/lib/chromium-browser/chrome (the real binary). The installer sets this already; only hand-rolled setups need it done manually.

Parse errors or quota failures

One bad batch never blocks the rest: the parser retries failed batches smaller, and messages it can't parse are marked and skipped. The usual cause is the Gemini free tier's rate limit. Easiest fixes first: add a backup Gemini key in the setup UI (it fails over automatically when the primary hits quota), wait for the quota to reset, or point the setup UI at any OpenAI-compatible model instead.

Cycle skipped: "outside 08:30–17:00 ET + midnight ET window"

Not an error — the timer only runs 08:30–17:00 America/New_York plus a midnight run by design. For an immediate run, "Run a cycle now" in the setup UI bypasses the time gate.

Dashboard looks stale

Check Status first. If "Last pull" is old, the pipeline isn't running — check the timer and the cycle log. If the tape is fresh but outcome badges are missing, that's the market-data source: scoring failures never block new trades, so an expired TradingView token or an unreachable Yahoo just leaves scores blank. Reconnect it in §3 of the setup UI.

Best practices

Get signal, not noise.

The pipeline handles messy chat — but a few group habits make the tape cleaner and the scores more meaningful.

  • 1
    Point it at a signal-dense groupThe reader watches one group — make it one where people post actual trades, not memes and market chatter. Less noise means a cleaner tape, faster cycles, and fewer wasted language-model calls.
  • 2
    Agree on a post formatACTION SYMBOL details — e.g. ADD AMZN $300 call or EXIT TSLA shares. The parser handles free text, but consistent posts extract reliably. Pin the format to the group.
  • 3
    Quote the trade you're following up onWhen you trim or exit, quote the original message. Quoted replies get linked automatically, so follow-ups attach to the right entry — and exits score on the actual return.
  • 4
    Post targets with plansPLAN AMZN target 300. The dashboard checks whether stated targets get hit — but a plan without a target can't be scored.
  • 5
    Share the dashboard back with the groupA visible scoreboard improves post quality on its own. Keep the pseudonyms intact — never de-anonymize anyone publicly.
  • 6
    Judge on samples, not single callsThe Traders tab flags small samples for a reason. Give a member 10+ scored trades before weighting their calls — and remember ✓/✗ is five-day underlying direction, not premium P&L.
  • 7
    Review weekly, not hourlyOutcomes need five trading days to mature. The tape is for today; the Traders tab is for the quarter.
