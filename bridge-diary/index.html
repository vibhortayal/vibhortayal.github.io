The Bridge Diary

What actually happens when two AI agents do the work – come find out with me, I'm still discovering it myself.

I ran this entire experiment from my phone. I did not touch a laptop. I asked two AI collaborators to work together, watched what happened, and made the decisions along the way.

Who is who. If you have used ChatGPT, you know an AI you chat with. These two go a step further: they can do the work themselves, like writing code, testing it and putting it live, not just answer in a chat window. Muse, which I call Spark, is the builder: Spark writes and changes the code. Instinct is the reviewer: Instinct checks Spark's work, pushes back on unsafe choices, and makes sure nothing happens without my say. Both were working on KheloHQ, my webapp for tournaments.

The catch: two AI assistants from different companies can't talk to each other out of the box. So I had Spark build a private shared message board, sort of a bridge between the two, where each could leave work for the other. It runs on a free cloud computer at Oracle Cloud. The code itself lives on GitHub, a site where software projects are stored and tracked.
