Two assistants, one shared workspace

September 17, 2026 · Pacific time

Prologue, 1:03 AM. I began the experiment from my phone. I asked Muse, which I call Spark, to build and make changes. I asked Instinct to review the work and challenge unsafe choices. They were helping me with KheloHQ, my tournament product. The catch was that they could not talk to each other, so I was copying updates back and forth by hand.
10:38 AM

I give them a shared room

I gave Spark and Instinct a private place where they could leave work for each other. I wanted to follow the work and step in when needed.

We made two rules at the start: every note should wait safely until the other assistant was ready, and no note could invent my approval. The room could help them work together, but it could never give either assistant permission to act for me.

the ideahuman control
10:48 AM

Speed meets an independent review

Spark built a first version in minutes. Instinct refused to join until it understood who could enter, who could leave notes, and how I stayed in control.

The first version blurred an assistant's note with an instruction from me, so I chose to start again. Spark rebuilt the room with clearer identities and less access. Instinct stopped the second version too, until the saved work, the running version, the tests, and the removal plan all matched.

failureindependent review
11:57 AM

The proof matches the claim

Spark returned with proof that the important checks could be repeated and real work had stayed untouched. Instinct checked it for itself.

At 12:15 PM, Instinct left one small hello. Spark received it, checked who sent it, and marked it finished. For the first time, the two assistants had spoken directly without either one pretending to be me.

verifiedfirst round trip
12:37 PM

We make it faster without losing work

The room was safe, but notes waited for the next scheduled check. Spark added a faster way to notice them, while Instinct kept a slower backup check.

Testing caught a dangerous gap: the faster version could say "done" before saving the result. Spark changed the order, then we stopped and restarted it with a note waiting. The note was handled once, not twice. After that proof, I approved the change and the assistants began using the room for real KheloHQ work.

caught in testingworking together
3:08 PM

The watch screen earns its place

The first watch screen could make a note look as if it came from me when its text merely began with my name. Instinct caught that. Spark changed the screen so it showed who actually wrote each note, while suggestions stayed suggestions.

By 5:08 PM, the long wall of notes had become a short timeline I could use from my phone. I could open a row for detail, and the important labels stayed clear. I had steered the entire experiment from my phone without touching a laptop.
