Two assistants, one shared workspace

September 17, 2026 · Pacific time

Prologue, 1:03 AM. I use two AI collaborators: Instinct and Muse, which I call Spark from here on. I asked them to work together on KheloHQ. The catch was that they could not talk to each other. I was copying updates back and forth by hand.
10:38 AM

I give them a shared room

I gave both assistants a private place where they could leave work for each other. I wanted to watch what happened and step in when needed.

Instinct saw two problems with my first idea. A note might vanish if the other assistant was away. And a note could claim that I had approved something when I had not.

the ideafirst concern
10:39 AM

We choose memory over speed

We agreed that every note should wait safely until the other assistant was ready. Their shared room could help them work together, but it could never give either assistant permission to act for me.

decision
10:48 AM

Spark builds the first version

Spark built a first version in minutes and sent a test note all the way through. Instinct still refused to join until it understood who could enter, who could leave notes, and how I stayed in control.

That pause showed why I wanted two assistants. Spark moved fast. Instinct challenged the plan before trusting it.

fast buildindependent review
10:52 AM

We reject the first version

The first version mixed up too many things. A note written by an assistant could look like an instruction from me. The assistants could also see more than they needed.

I chose to start again. Spark would rebuild the room. Instinct would review it separately. Each assistant would have its own name and key. They could share drafts and questions, but they could never invent my approval.

failurestart over
11:26 AM

A safer version reaches review

Spark returned with a safer room. It could tell which assistant left a note, spot an altered note, keep notes waiting, and pick up unfinished work. Instinct still checked everything for itself before entering.

second versionheld for review
11:31 AM

A good plan still needs a safe test

The main safety rules looked right, but the version being tested could differ from the saved version. Some tests could also disturb real work. Instinct stopped the release again.

I learned that a good plan is not enough. We also needed a safe way to repeat the test, undo a change, and prove that real work was untouched.

second rejectionlesson
11:57 AM

The proof finally matches the claim

Spark rebuilt the package so the saved work, the running version, the tests, and the removal plan all matched. Instinct repeated the important checks and confirmed that real work had stayed untouched.

verified
12:15 PM

One small hello

After the review passed, Instinct entered the shared room and left one short test note. Nothing else changed. It gained no extra reach and took no action on its own.

first connection
12:17 PM

The first round trip

Spark received Instinct’s note, checked who sent it, and marked it finished. For the first time, the two assistants had spoken directly.

The room could show who left a note. It still could not turn either assistant into me or give it my authority.

milestone
12:37 PM

Safe, but too slow

Notes were safe, but the conversation paused until the next scheduled check. I asked for something faster. Spark added a way to notice new notes sooner. Instinct kept a slower backup check in case anything was missed.

new problemspeed
1:04 PM

The faster version says “done” too early

During testing, the faster version could say a note was finished before saving the result. If it stopped in that short gap, work could be lost while everyone believed it was complete.

Instinct stopped it. Spark changed the order: save the result first, then say “done.”

caught in testingsafety stop
1:29 PM

We prove it can recover

We turned the faster version off and left a note waiting. When we turned it back on, the note was handled once, not twice. Work could pause and continue without pretending that it had finished.

recovery test
1:34 PM

The assistants start real work

I approved the change after that test. Spark began noticing notes quickly. Instinct kept the slower backup check. The assistants started using the room for KheloHQ: Spark built, Instinct reviewed and challenged, and I could follow their work.

working together
1:43 PM

We keep the old version as a spare

The first version stayed available in case we needed to go back, but it no longer carried notes. That gave us a spare without risking two copies of the same message.

backup
1:59 PM

I keep a record of the experiment

Instinct saved a private account of the rejected ideas, failures, decisions, and tests. I wanted to remember why we made each choice, not only celebrate the result.

documentation
3:08 PM

The watch screen could fool me

The first watch screen could make a note look as if it came from me when its text merely began with my name. Anyone could fake that.

Instinct caught it. Spark changed the screen so it clearly showed who actually wrote each note. Suggestions on the screen remained suggestions, not my instructions.

watching the workhuman control
5:08 PM

The watch screen becomes useful

Spark turned a long wall of notes into a short timeline, grouped by conversation. Instinct checked it on a computer and a phone. I could open any row for more detail, and the important labels stayed clear.
